# SanDisk (SNDK) & Western Digital (WDC) — Q4 FY26 Earnings Breakdown

**Reported:** August 5, 2026, after the close (both, same day — fiscal year ended July 3, 2026)
**Analysis date:** August 5, 2026 (post-market)

> Sources: WDC Q4FY26 earnings deck (Aug 5, 2026), Sandisk fiscal Q4 2026 press release, IBKR market data, and press coverage listed at the bottom. Consensus figures vary by aggregator and are labelled where they conflict. This is analysis, not investment advice.

---

## 1. The one-paragraph version

Both companies printed blowout quarters. SanDisk beat consensus revenue by ~6% and EPS by ~12%, with an **84.6% gross margin** — a number that does not exist in normal semiconductor history. Western Digital beat on both lines, expanded gross margin 1,310bps year-over-year, and guided Q1 FY27 slightly *above* consensus. Both stocks were destroyed anyway: **SNDK -11.7%** and **WDC -15.5%** from the prior close, including after-hours. The reason is not the results. It is that (a) both stocks had already de-rated ~40-55% from their late-June highs and then squeezed violently higher into the print, (b) two-thirds of the sequential growth at *both* companies came from price rather than volume, and (c) SanDisk's Q1 guide broke the beat-and-raise streak — the first quarter of the cycle where sequential gross margin is guided flat-to-down. The market has stopped asking how high pricing goes and started asking when it rolls.

### The contracted-revenue structure (NBM) — the genuinely new thing

- **10 New Business Model agreements across 8 customers**
- **$93.9B minimum contracted revenue at floor pricing**
- **$16.5B in financial guarantees** (cash deposits and instruments)
- **>50% of FY27 bits committed**, ~two-thirds of FY28 bits
- Weighted average duration **>4 years**
- RPO $59.8B at quarter end, **$91.1B pro forma** including post-quarter signings

This is a structural change to how NAND is sold. A floor-price, multi-year, cash-collateralised take-or-pay contract is closer to a utility contract than a commodity spot sale. Three quarters ago this was $42B across 3 agreements; it is now $93.9B across 10.

### Supply discipline

Management guided **mid-to-high-teens bit production growth** with **capex at ~6% of revenue** (FY26 capex was $177M on $20.2B — under 1%, though the JV structure understates true capital intensity). They are explicitly choosing not to volume-chase.

## 5. Why it corrected so heavily

**1. The correction started six weeks before the print.** Both stocks peaked June 18–25. The semiconductor complex shed roughly $1.5 trillion in market value from June 25 onward, and memory specifically entered a bear market. Four catalysts stacked up:

- **July 13** — SK Hynix guided 2026 capex up ~50% to at least $31B. The single most reliable bear signal in memory is the incumbent deciding to spend.
- **July 24** — a Korean chip selloff dragged the US memory names down 6–9%.
- **July 27** — **China's CXMT held its IPO**, funding another wave of domestic memory capacity, alongside reports that **Apple is testing CXMT DRAM**. That compresses the timeline on Chinese supply reaching tier-1 customers.
- **Late July** — evidence the HBM shortage was easing as everyone added capacity.

By July 29, SNDK was down 56.5% from its high and WDC 38.1%. The earnings drop is the second leg, not the first.

**2. The setup into the print was maximally fragile.** From the July 29 trough, SNDK ran **+40.5% in four sessions** and WDC **+18.7%**. On August 4 alone SanDisk jumped 8%, Micron 6%, on price-target hikes and the SNDK/SK Hynix High Bandwidth Flash standard announcement. The options market was pricing a **12–25% implied move** on SNDK. So the print was met by freshly-added, levered longs sitting on a four-day squeeze with a massive event premium about to be crushed. Actual moves of -11.7% and -15.5% are *inside* what options were pricing — this was a priced-in-magnitude event, not a shock.

**3. SanDisk's specific break: the guide, and the margin.**
- Revenue guide midpoint **$10.55B vs $10.82B consensus (-2.5%)**. After a quarter that beat their own guidance by 12% and consensus by 6%, "in line to slightly light" is functionally a cut. When a stock is up 430% YTD, meeting expectations is a downgrade.
- **Gross margin guided 83.0–84.9% vs 84.6% delivered — the first sequential non-expansion of the entire cycle.** For a stock whose whole thesis is pricing-driven margin expansion, that is the tell. Opex is also guided +22.7% sequentially against revenue +17.7%.

**4. The quality of the growth is the real problem — at both companies.**
- SanDisk: ~two-thirds of the $3.0B sequential revenue increase came from **price**, one-third from volume. Incremental gross margin was **96.8%** ($2.92B of gross profit on $3.02B of incremental revenue). Nothing sustains a 97% incremental margin.
- Western Digital: identical structure — exabytes +4.1%, price +7.9%.
- Price is the component that mean-reverts. Volume is the component that compounds. The market re-weighted toward the mean-reverting half.
- And **SanDisk Consumer revenue fell 32% sequentially** — the demand destruction is already visible.
- Supply discipline (mid-to-high-teens bit growth, capex ~6% of revenue) is the bull case *and* the bear case: it supports pricing, but it means the company structurally cannot grow on volume. If price flattens, revenue flattens.

**5. Western Digital fell harder despite a cleaner print, and the reason is arithmetic.** WDC beat by 1.4% on revenue and guided 1.5% above consensus. Nothing was wrong. But at the June 18 peak WDC carried a **~$290B market cap at ~47x annualised run-rate EPS** — for a hard-disk-drive manufacturer doing $12.9B of annual revenue. Even after a 38% decline it trades at **29x forward run-rate and ~35x annualised FCF versus SanDisk's 7x and ~9.5x**. WDC has more than four times SanDisk's multiple on run-rate earnings. When a stock is priced for permanence and delivers 12% sequential growth decelerating to a guided 9.4%, a 1.4% beat cannot hold the multiple.

**6. The single most important structural point: SNDK at 7x forward run-rate earnings is not cheap — it is textbook.** Memory equities peak when the P/E is *lowest* and earnings are still accelerating, because the market discounts the cycle top 6–9 months ahead of the earnings top. A 7x multiple on peak-cycle EPS is the market saying "these earnings are not the base." The de-rating happens *through* rising earnings. That is precisely the pattern in progress.

**7. And critically — the peers barely moved.** On August 5, **Micron closed -0.96%** and **Seagate -1.82%**, versus -11.7% and -15.5% for SNDK and WDC. The market read these prints as company-specific valuation and positioning events, **not** as evidence of a demand break. That is a meaningful distinction for the sector read-through.

## 6. What it means for semis and memory going forward

### The cycle is not over, but the multiple cycle is

The fundamentals in these two reports are extraordinary and still improving sequentially: SanDisk guides revenue +18% QoQ and EPS +15% QoQ; WDC guides revenue +9% and margins *up again*. Nobody's business is deteriorating. What has changed is that the market will no longer pay an expanding multiple for it. Expect the classic memory pattern: **earnings keep rising while the stocks chop sideways-to-lower with violent volatility** — SNDK's annualised implied vol is ~119% and WDC's ~99%.

### Supply is the whole game from here

Every incremental datapoint that matters is a supply datapoint:
- SK Hynix capex +50% to ≥$31B — already in the market.
- CXMT newly IPO'd and funded, with Apple qualifying its DRAM.
- SanDisk holding bit growth to mid-to-high-teens at ~6% of revenue capex.
- Samsung's posture, which is the historic swing factor.

At 84.6% gross margins the economic incentive to add capacity is overwhelming. The discipline holds only as long as every player believes every other player is disciplined. **The first credible capacity announcement from Samsung or a Chinese producer is the event that ends this.**

### NAND vs. HDD are diverging structurally, and the market is mispricing which one is safer

- **HDD** is a three-player oligopoly (WDC, Seagate, Toshiba) with **no Chinese entrant**, real capacity constraints in heads and media, LTAs extending to 2031, 25%+ exabyte growth, and a HAMR roadmap (44TB → 50TB) that is genuinely hard to replicate. This is the more defensible supply structure.
- **NAND** has six-plus suppliers, an active Chinese entrant in YMTC, and a technology roadmap that scales layers relatively predictably.
- Yet **WDC trades at 29x run-rate and SNDK at 7x**. The market is paying a 4x multiple premium for the more defensible supply structure — which is directionally right and quite possibly excessive in magnitude. The valuation risk sits with WDC; the estimate risk sits with SNDK.

### SanDisk's NBM structure is the most underappreciated item in either release

$93.9B of minimum contracted revenue at floor pricing, $16.5B of cash and instrument guarantees, >50% of FY27 bits and ~two-thirds of FY28 bits committed, 4+ year weighted duration. If those contracts hold, **a meaningful share of FY27–28 revenue is insulated from spot price collapse.** That materially changes the downside distribution for the next NAND downturn versus every prior cycle — and it is the strongest argument that 7x is too cheap. The bear counter is that floor prices are floors, not fixed prices: in a downturn realised pricing falls to the floor and the upside optionality disappears, so the contracts compress the distribution in both directions. Watch counterparty concentration — eight customers is not many.

### What to watch next, in order

1. **SanDisk Investor Day, August 13** — eight days out. Management sets the FY27 framework, bit growth, capex and the long-term margin model. This is the near-term catalyst and the reason SNDK stays volatile regardless of the print.
2. **Monthly NAND and DRAM contract prices** (TrendForce) — the second derivative is now the entire debate.
3. **Korean semiconductor export data** — the highest-frequency read on memory pricing and volume.
4. **Micron's fiscal Q4, late September** — the sector's next real test.
5. **Any Samsung or CXMT capacity announcement** — the thing that ends the cycle.
6. **Whether SanDisk's Consumer weakness spreads into Edge** — Edge is 61% of revenue and includes smartphones and PCs. Consumer rolling over is tolerable; Edge rolling over is not.

### Bottom line

The results were excellent and the guidance is fine. What broke is the willingness to pay a rising multiple for price-driven earnings when supply is visibly being added. Both stocks are now discounting a cycle top that has not yet arrived in the numbers — which is exactly what memory equities always do. The differentiated calls out of this print are that **SanDisk's contracted-revenue structure genuinely reduces downside variance in a way no prior NAND cycle had**, that **WDC's 29x forward multiple is the more exposed of the two**, and that **semicap is the cleanest beneficiary of the capacity response these margins are guaranteeing.**
