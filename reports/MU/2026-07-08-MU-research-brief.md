## PART A — FUNDAMENTAL RESEARCH BRIEF

### INVESTMENT THESIS

- **The steepest earnings ramp in large-cap history, and it is contracted, not spot.** FQ3-26 (ended May 28, 2026) revenue was $41.46B, up 346% YoY and 74% sequentially; non-GAAP EPS $25.11; FQ4 guidance is $50B ± $1B revenue, ~86% gross margin, $31 ± $1 non-GAAP EPS (8-K, June 24, 2026). Management cited 16 multi-year Strategic Customer Agreements (SCAs) — including a $22B non-cancelable Anthropic supply deal signed June 22 — with remaining performance obligations (RPO, contracted future revenue) of roughly $100B per the FQ3 earnings call. Note: the $100B RPO figure is from the call/prepared remarks, not the 8-K itself.
- **Fortress balance sheet at the point of maximum cycle risk.** $30.2B cash and investments against $5.72B total debt — ~$24.5B net cash — with FQ3 operating cash flow of $25.39B and adjusted free cash flow of $18.3B after $7.1B capex (8-K, June 24, 2026). Micron is self-funding record capex (~$10B guided for FQ4) without leverage, which is what breaks memory companies in downturns.
- **The market is already pricing the cycle roll.** At ~$920 the stock trades at ~12.6× FY26E non-GAAP EPS of ~$73 (three reported quarters plus Q4 guide midpoint) and ~7.4× the FQ4 guide annualized — versus ~20.5× trailing. The stock is down ~26% from its June 25 all-time high ($1,255 intraday) despite guidance being raised. The debate is not FY26; it is whether FY27 earnings hold. The ~$100B contracted RPO is the strongest argument any memory company has ever had that they will.

### BUSINESS MODEL

Micron makes DRAM (dynamic random-access memory — the working memory in servers, PCs, phones) and NAND (persistent flash storage), sold as chips, modules, HBM (high-bandwidth memory — DRAM die stacked and co-packaged next to AI accelerators), and SSDs. Since FY26 it reports four units; FQ3-26 revenue split (8-K, June 24, 2026): Cloud Memory $13.77B (83% gross margin), Core Data Center $11.52B (87%), Mobile & Client $11.52B (87%), Automotive & Embedded $4.63B (79%). Data-center-exposed revenue exceeded $25B — over 60% of the company.

**Moat.** Commodity DRAM/NAND is an oligopoly with weak pricing power in normal times; the current moat rests on three harder-to-replicate legs: (1) HBM — packaging/stacking complexity and 12–18 month customer qualification cycles make share sticky within a product generation; (2) the SCA structure, which converts spot-price exposure into multi-year, non-cancelable contracted volume — genuinely new for this industry; (3) Micron is the only US-headquartered DRAM maker, with CHIPS Act-supported domestic fabs, which matters for US hyperscaler and government procurement.

**Competitive position.** DRAM revenue share (Q1 2026, Omdia via TechTimes): Samsung 38.6%, SK Hynix 28.8%, Micron 22.4%. HBM share (Q2 2026, Counterpoint/industry trackers): SK Hynix ~62%, Micron ~21%, Samsung ~17% — Micron has overtaken Samsung for #2 in HBM but remains a third the size of SK Hynix there. All three memory makers invested in Anthropic's Series H alongside supply agreements.

**Fair value range: $1,050–$1,300 (12-month).** Reasoning: FY27 scenarios — (a) base: revenue plateaus near the FQ4 exit rate (~$200B annualized) then softens in 2H as Samsung/SK Hynix capacity lands, gross margin compresses from 86% toward 65–70%, EPS ~$105–120; at a peak-aware 9–11× that's ~$950–$1,300. (b) Bull: SCAs hold pricing, EPS $140+, 10× → $1,400+. (c) Bear: classic roll, EPS ~$55–65, 10× → ~$600. Weighting base heavily and bear more than bull (memory supply responses have never failed to arrive) centers fair value near $1,150.

### CATALYSTS

1. **SK Hynix Nasdaq listing, ~July 10, 2026** — near-term rotation/supply-of-paper risk (widely cited as a driver of the current pullback); medium-term, a US-listed pure-play comp could re-rate the whole memory group.
2. **FQ4-26 earnings, ~September 21–22, 2026** (aggregator estimate — Investing.com/Nasdaq; not yet company-confirmed, verify nearer the date). Bar: $50B ± $1B revenue, ~86% GM, $31 ± $1 EPS, plus the first FY27 framing. Given the pattern of guidance beats, the FY27 Q1 guide matters more than the Q4 print.
3. **HBM4 / 16-Hi qualification and NVIDIA allocation decisions, 2H CY26** — trade press (TweakTown) reports all three vendors competing for next-gen allocation; share shifts here move the FY27 model directly.
4. **Additional SCA signings / RPO updates** — the ~$100B RPO baseline from the FQ3 call is the single most important number to track quarter over quarter.
5. **Monthly DRAM/NAND contract pricing (TrendForce)** — and specifically the July 2 TrendForce report that SK Hynix removed price caps in its long-term agreements while Micron's remain capped: bullish for reported industry pricing, but a relative ASP-capture disadvantage for Micron if spot keeps rising.

### RISKS

- **Supply response (the mechanism that ends every memory cycle).** Samsung and SK Hynix are both scaling DRAM/HBM capacity through 2026–27 (DataCenterDynamics). When bit supply catches demand, pricing rolls; Micron's guided 86% gross margin compares with a prior-cycle peak of ~61% (FY2018). At $50B/quarter revenue, every 10 points of gross margin is ~$5B of quarterly operating income — the earnings downside from margin normalization alone is enormous, independent of volumes.
- **SK Hynix specifically.** It holds ~62% HBM share, has just uncapped pricing in its LTAs while Micron's are capped, and lists on Nasdaq July 10. The mechanism: SK Hynix captures more of any further price upside, reports optically superior ASP momentum, and competes for the same US investor dollars.
- **Vendor-financing circularity.** Micron invested in Anthropic's Series H while simultaneously signing a $22B supply agreement with it (Blocks & Files, June 23). If frontier-lab capex slows, contracted RPO with AI-lab counterparties is only as durable as those counterparties' funding.
- **China/CXMT.** CXMT capacity additions pressure commodity DDR pricing at the low end (industry DRAM reports), eroding the non-HBM ~40% of Micron's business first.
- **The value-trap pattern.** A memory stock at ~20× trailing peak earnings is historically *expensive*, not cheap — low trailing multiples appear at tops. A reported Michael Burry short position (Forbes) is a sentiment marker of exactly this read, not evidence in itself.

**Invalidation triggers (observable, specific):** (1) two consecutive months of DRAM contract price *declines* in TrendForce data; (2) FY27 Q1 guidance implying sequential revenue decline, or any gross-margin guide below ~80%; (3) Micron HBM share falling below ~20% in quarterly tracker data; (4) RPO flat or down quarter-over-quarter; (5) an SCA counterparty renegotiating or defaulting. Any two of these together kills the thesis.

### VERDICT

**Buy. 12-month price target: $1,150** (midpoint of the $1,050–$1,300 fair-value range).

Reasoning chain: FY26E EPS ~$73 is in hand (three quarters reported, Q4 guided with a company that has over-delivered its own guides three straight quarters). The stock at ~$920 discounts an FY27 earnings decline of roughly 40–50% from the FQ4 run-rate. The ~$100B non-cancelable RPO, net-cash balance sheet, and HBM qualification stickiness make that steep a decline the *bear* case, not the base case. A base-case FY27 EPS of ~$105–120 on a deliberately conservative 9–11× peak-aware multiple yields $1,050–$1,300. The position should be sized for 10%-per-day volatility, and the invalidation triggers above are the exit discipline — not the price target.

## PART C — FUNDAMENTAL/TECHNICAL ALIGNMENT

The two briefs conflict, and the conflict is informative. Fundamentally this is a **Buy** on a 12-month horizon: FY26 earnings are contractually underwritten to a degree no memory cycle has ever been, the balance sheet is net cash, and the stock discounts a steep FY27 rollover. Technically the daily chart says **TRIM**: the June 25 all-time high was made on divergent momentum and non-confirming volume flow, distribution is visible in the tape, and price is only one ~$40 slip from breaking its structural shelf.

These are not contradictory — they operate on different clocks. **The technical call governs entry timing; the fundamental call governs direction and sizing.** Practically: existing holders lighten into the $1,000–1,035 shelf and hold a core against the $880 close-basis line; incremental buyers wait for either (a) a successful high-volume reclaim of $1,035, or (b) the 50% retracement near $783–800 — where the fundamental Buy and a technical support structure would finally point the same way. What nobody should do is initiate a full position mid-shelf three days before the SK Hynix listing with OBV still under its signal.
