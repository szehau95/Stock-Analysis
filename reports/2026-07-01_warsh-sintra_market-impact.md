## 1. Speech reconstruction  [FACT]

- **(a) Inflation / price stability:** "We're all in the price stability business… we've all looked around, and we've seen that prices are too high." If households/businesses expect the Fed to tolerate >2%, "I guess they'd be disappointed." 2% target reaffirmed as non-negotiable.
- **(b) Rate path / reaction function:** **Declined to hint at the July decision.** No forward guidance, consistent with the June FOMC posture. Framed policy as data-dependent; effectively pushed back on the rate cuts the White House has sought.
- **(c) Forward guidance & communication:** Continuation of the "less-is-more" communication doctrine — panel format, no signal. Reiterated the **five task forces** (data, comms, inflation framework, AI/productivity, etc.).
- **(d) Balance sheet:** Continuity; secondary to rates. (Consistent with prior "borders on fiscal policy" framing — no new news at Sintra.)
- **(e) Growth / labor / AI:** Open-minded that **AI expands supply and is disinflationary over time**, but "not enough to shift stance." Flagged that persistent-inflation risk has **moderated** — cited declining survey- and bond-implied inflation expectations over the past month.

**Takeaway:** He said nothing new on rates on purpose; the one genuine tell is acknowledging expectations have cooled — a marginal, not-yet-actionable dovish crumb inside an unchanged hawkish frame.

## 7. Positioning calls (ranked, decisive)

**Guardrail applied:** A macro speech reprices the discount rate; it does not break a thesis. Rate-driven multiple compression on a name owned on fundamentals is a hold/add, not an exit. But **2× daily-reset leverage is a different animal** — it is not a compounder, it decays in chop, and it is a trading vehicle, not a core hold. So the calls below separate "hedge the macro" from "manage the leverage."

1. **DO NOT panic-sell MUU/RAM into today's print — HOLD the thesis.** *Rationale:* today is idiosyncratic (antitrust headline + SK Hynix supply overhang + Q3 rebalancing), not a Fed-driven thesis break; memory-chip prices were actually *higher* in June. *Trigger to revisit:* a genuine DRAM pricing rollover or an adverse antitrust ruling, not a one-day gap. *Confidence: Medium-High.*
2. **Actively manage the 2× leverage — this is the real action item.** *Rationale:* MUU/RAM are daily-reset leveraged ETFs; in a higher-for-longer, high-vol regime they bleed to path/decay even if MU round-trips. Consider converting *part* of the leveraged exposure to **unlevered MU** to keep the thesis while killing the decay and halving the gap risk. *Trigger:* size the trim to your risk budget, not to Warsh. *Confidence: High.*
3. **De-concentrate, don't de-risk the view.** *Rationale:* ~95% of the book in one 2× memory trade is the dominant portfolio risk. Trimming *leverage/size* (not conviction) is prudent regardless of the Fed. *Confidence: High.*
4. **Hedge the macro separately from the book.** *Rationale:* if you want to neutralize the higher-for-longer discount-rate risk, do it with a macro overlay (e.g. long USD / short-duration or a QQQ hedge), not by selling your compounder thesis. *Confidence: Medium.*
5. **IBIT — hold, it's working as the diversifier.** No action; too small to matter either way. *Confidence: Medium.*
