### The common backdrop: rates

These pressures hit every bank in late September and early October.

**The Fed.** It raised rates 25bp on 16/09/2026, its first hike in more than three years (The Star, 22/09). It held on 28/10 (Malay Mail, 29/10).

**Malaysian government bond yields.** The 10-year MGS:

| Date | 10-year MGS |
|---|---|
| 11/09 | 3.60% |
| Peak | 4.18% |
| 13/10 | 3.83% |
| 29/10 | 3.93% |

Source: BNM, T1. The average 3-to-10-year yield is still about 30bp above its 30/06 level. Bond-book losses for the July–September quarter will therefore show up when banks report in late November.

**JPMorgan, 07/10.** JPMorgan cut CIMB to Underweight from Neutral and AMMB to Neutral from Overweight. Its argument:
- rising long-dated yields erode trading gains and capital, and could affect dividends;
- funding costs rise;
- third-quarter surprises are "likely skewed negative".

On the day, CIMB fell 4.81% to RM7.33 and AMMB fell 4.48%. The Bursa financial index hit a 10-month low (The Edge, 07/10).

**Regional spillover.** The next day, banks sold off across Southeast Asia, with DBS down 5.2% (The Edge/Bloomberg, 08/10).

**BNM policy meeting, 05/11.** The OPR is 2.75%. ANZ and UOB have called a 25bp hike (The Edge, 17/09). A hike cuts both ways for banks: wider loan margins, but further losses on bond holdings.

Most banks have recovered since mid-October. Maybank and Public Bank are flat on the month. CIMB has kept falling.

### Why CIMB fell so hard: four shocks, and it had the least cushion against each

**1. It has the biggest bond trading book among Malaysian banks.**
- Securities are 29% of CIMB's assets. Its trading book, marked through profit and loss (FVTPL), is 27% of its debt securities, the highest in Malaysia (JPMorgan via The Edge, 07/10).
- On CIMB's own 30/06/2026 statements (T1):
  - FVTPL holdings are RM66.7bn, of which RM46.3bn is government paper.
  - FVOCI debt (marked through equity) is RM83.7bn.
- The cumulative revaluation reserve on the FVOCI book was already **negative RM451m** at 30/06. With no gains left to absorb further losses, every additional loss hits CET1 capital in full under BNM's capital framework.
- Each +100bp rise in yields costs about 52bp of CET1 and 2.9% of book value.

**2. Indonesia, 16/10.**
- On 15/10, MSCI launched a consultation on reclassifying Indonesia from Emerging Market to Standalone status (MSCI release; Reuters via NST, 16/10). Jakarta's index fell about 11% the next day.
- On 16/10, CIMB fell 3.74% to RM7.20 on 3.7× its normal volume. The median of the other five banks rose 0.55%, and the KLCI fell 0.98%.
- CIMB Niaga (91.45% owned) is the most Indonesia-exposed asset among the Malaysian banks:
  - On the 1H26 run-rate, Niaga is about **18% of group net profit** (my estimate, derived from Niaga's reported ROE path).
  - Niaga holds 13% of group gross loans (T1).
- Indonesia's new free-float rule forces CIMB to cut its stake to 87.5% or less by March 2027, and 85% or less by March 2028 (The Edge, 29/10).
- Analysts said after a CFO briefing that privatisation was being studied (The Edge, 21/10). CIMB denied any decision or formal proposal on 22/10 (cimb.com, FMT).
- The market is left guessing the capital cost. Public Investment Bank (PIVB) estimates about RM0.8bn for a full buyout, roughly 20bp of CET1.

**3. Niaga's nine-month results, 28/10 after the close.**
- Net profit fell 14.7% to IDR4.49tn (about RM1.01bn).
- Provisions rose to IDR2.37tn from IDR1.26tn.
- Net interest income fell 2.2% (The Edge, 29/10).
- Kenanga's read (BusinessToday, 29/10):
  - Niaga's third-quarter ROE was **7.6%**;
  - nine-month credit cost was **135bp**;
  - net interest margin fell 18bp to 3.82%.
- Kenanga cut its CIMB target to **RM7.90** (Outperform kept) and trimmed FY26/27 earnings forecasts by 2.4% and 2.6%.
- On 29/10, CIMB opened 3 sen higher at RM7.14 (Bernama), then closed **−3.09% at RM6.89** on 2× volume, while peers rose.
- By my estimate, Niaga's third quarter alone takes about **7% off group quarterly net profit** compared with its first-half run-rate.

**4. Forecast cuts and selling by EPF, Malaysia's state pension fund.**
- Around 21/10, four houses cut targets:

  | Maybank IB | Hold | RM8.40 → RM7.40 | Indonesia |

  Source: The Edge, 21/10.

- CIMB is also running a sale of its Philippine unit. Bids from BPI and Metrobank are due by end-October, with a value of up to US$200m (Bloomberg via The Star, 28/10). This is a simplification positive, but too small to change the picture.

## Part 2: Research note — CIMB Group Holdings (CIMB MK), HOLD, 12-month price target RM7.25

### INVESTMENT THESIS

- **The de-rating is earned, not an overreaction.**
  - CIMB is the Malaysian bank most exposed to both of this quarter's shocks, rising yields and Indonesia, and it entered them with the thinnest buffers:
    - a negative FVOCI reserve (−RM451m);
    - CET1 of 13.91% after the proposed dividend, against management's at-least-14% target (the results release rounds it to 14.0%);
    - about 18% of earnings in a subsidiary whose quarterly ROE just fell from about 12.6% to 7.6%.
  - Price-to-book has compressed from 1.37× (27/01/2026, the five-year high) to 1.06×.
- **At RM6.89 the market prices CIMB at about a 10.8% cost of equity against an ~11% ROE. That is fair value, not deep value.**
  - The stock trades at its five-year mean P/B (1.05×) and at the 54th percentile of its own five-year range.
  - The 5.8% ordinary dividend yield (6.8% including last year's 7-sen special) supports the downside. Bond-book and Niaga risk cap the upside until the third-quarter print proves them contained.
- **The 2027 re-rating case rests on capital, not earnings growth.**
  - 1H26 net profit was flat (−0.2% year on year), and the Forward30 target of 12–13% ROE by FY2027 looks out of reach from today's 11.0–11.2%.
  - The upside levers are capital:
    - the remaining RM0.85–1.3bn of the RM2bn capital-return pledge;
    - the Thai auto-loan disposal;
    - Philippine sale proceeds.
  - Against these sits the cost of fixing Niaga's free float (roughly 20bp of CET1 if CIMB buys out minorities).

The Digital Assets & Group Funding segment includes the Touch 'n Go Digital joint venture. Its payment value rose 68.8% to RM157bn, and the group's share of JV profit was RM82m in 1H26.

**Moat**
- A large, diversified deposit franchise: group CASA 42.6%; Niaga CASA 72%.
- Wholesale and treasury scale that few domestic peers match.
- A Shariah-compliant business producing 22% of income.
- The TNG e-wallet, a distribution asset competitors cannot easily replicate.

**Where CIMB sits against peers**
- On quality, it sits between Public Bank/Hong Leong Bank and RHB/AmBank.
- Its ROE (11.2% trailing) matches Maybank's and HLB's. Its cost-to-income ratio (45.2% in 2Q26) is far above Public Bank's 35.1%.
- The structural weakness is that regional subsidiaries (Indonesia, Thailand) earn below the Malaysian franchise. Hence the current pruning: Thai auto loans sold to Krungsri, and the Philippines up for sale.

- CIMB is now priced like RHB/AmBank on P/E while delivering Maybank/HLB-level ROE. The discount to Maybank (0.25× of P/B) is the market's charge for bond-book and Indonesia risk.

**Own history.** Five-year P/B mean 1.05× (standard deviation 0.15). Range 0.79× (02/06/2023) to 1.37× (27/01/2026). Today: 1.06×, 54th percentile.

**Justified P/B** = (ROE − g) / (COE − g), with g = 4% and COE = 10-year MGS 3.93% + beta × 6% equity risk premium.
- CIMB's beta is 1.41 raw, 1.28 Blume-adjusted (three-year weekly vs KLCI). Its COE therefore ranges from 9.9% (beta of 1) to 11.6% (Blume).
- At 11% ROE, justified P/B ranges from 0.92× to 1.18×, with a 1.04× midpoint.
- The current price implies a 10.8% COE.

**12-month fair value**

Method: justified P/B × forward book value. Forward book value adds 16 months of retained earnings at a 55% payout, less scenario bond marks and currency losses.

### CATALYSTS (next 3–6 months)

1. **05/11/2026: BNM monetary policy meeting.**
   - A 25bp OPR hike would lift CIMB's Malaysian NIM. CIMB's per-25bp sensitivity is unavailable; the sector is typically 2–4bp.
   - It would also hurt only if MGS yields rise with it. On the negative reserve, each +10bp on the curve costs about 5bp of CET1.
2. **Late November 2026: third-quarter results.**
   - The press cites 25/11. This is not yet confirmed via Bursa.
   - Watch:
     - group ROE vs the 11.0–11.5% guidance;
     - treasury income;
     - group credit cost vs 35bp;
     - CET1 vs 14%;
     - the decision between a special dividend and buybacks.
   - The fourth quarter has started kinder for bond marks: the average 3-to-10-year MGS yield is about 7bp lower than on 30/09.
3. **Philippine sale.** Binding bids are due by end-October. Signing at or above book value would confirm management's capital-recycling discipline.
4. **Niaga free-float solution, needed before March 2027.**
   - A placement at a reasonable price, or a privatisation costing around RM0.8bn, would remove the overhang.
   - A forced discounted sell-down would not.
5. **Capital return.**
   - RM2bn pledged by end-2027. Remaining balance:
     - RM0.85bn according to The Edge (21/10), after RM446m of buybacks;
     - RM1.3bn according to HLIB and TA (02/09).
   - Plus capital released by the Thai auto-loan disposal. Brokers estimate THB11–13bn; MBSB says repatriation is lower than first hoped.

### RISKS: the bear case, and what would break the thesis

**Bond market.**
- A further +75bp on MGS cuts book value by about 14 sen (2.2%) and CET1 by about 39bp, to roughly 13.5%.
- That is below management's 14% target, and it would likely shrink the capital return and dividend upside.

**Indonesia.**
- Niaga's 135bp credit cost persists into 2027.
- Bank Indonesia has raised rates 100bp since May (5.75% held on 21/10). Deposits reprice within weeks, loans over months (CIMB Securities via NST, 27/10).
- Indonesian loan-to-deposit ratios are tight: 92.7% industry-wide in August.
- MSCI's final decision is due by end-June 2027. Rupiah weakness feeds straight into book value through currency translation (−RM1.2bn in 1H26 alone).

**Earnings.** ROE slips to about 10%, below the FY26 guidance floor, and the market prices CIMB back to its 0.79× five-year low.

**Flows.**
- Continued EPF trimming.
- A high beta of 1.41 raw means CIMB falls further than the market in risk-off phases.

**Bear value: RM5.25** (−24%; −19% after a year of dividends).

**What would push me to Buy:**
- a third-quarter print with Niaga provisions clearly peaking and group ROE at 11% or above;
- MGS stable at or below 3.9% with CET1 at 14% or above;
- a confirmed special dividend or buyback tranche.

### VERDICT: HOLD. 12-month price target RM7.25.

**The arithmetic**
- The probability-weighted value of RM7.27 (rounded to RM7.25) is 5.2% above RM6.89.
- Add about 39 sen of ordinary dividends (5.7%), and the expected 12-month total return is about 11%.
- That roughly equals the 10.8% cost of equity: no excess return for the risk.

**The rule (set before computing)**
- Buy if the expected total return exceeds COE + 5pp.
- Reduce if it is negative.
- Hold otherwise.

**Price bands**
- **Accumulate below about RM6.60.** There, the expected return clears the Buy hurdle.
- **Reduce above about RM7.65.**

**Technical note.** The stock is oversold and a reflex bounce is plausible. The trend stays down until CIMB reclaims its 50-day EMA (about RM7.46 dividend-adjusted), so do not chase strength before the 25/11 print.
