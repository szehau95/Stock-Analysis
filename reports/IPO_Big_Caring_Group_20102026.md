- **Governance:** the chairman is Tan Sri Dr Noor Hisham Abdullah, former Director-General of Health. Creador sits on the board.

## 2. What you are buying

- **Malaysia's largest pharmacy chain.** It has 668 outlets at 30/09/2026 (626 at the draft's last practicable date), a 22.6% revenue share (2024, IMR), and is the only chain present in all states. Brands: BIG, CARiNG, Georgetown, Wellings and Ting. It has 7.2m members, its own manufacturing (Medispec), distribution, and physio/tele-consult services.
- **Structural tailwinds.** The population is ageing, and the IMR projects organised chains growing at a 10.9% CAGR from 2025 to 2030, against 7.7% for the overall market.
- **Track record, mostly built by acquisition:**

| **Balance sheet** | IPO cash cuts net bank debt / EBITDA from 2.6× to ~1.0× | Goodwill and intangibles of **RM1.34bn exceed equity**, so **NTA is negative** even after the IPO (~−RM0.16bn EST). The yield is 1.3%, and FCF yield was 3.3% on FY25 | At RM1.08 the market pays for the base case, with no margin of safety on fundamentals |
| **Regulation** | Medicine price display (PU(A) 141/2025) pushes dispensing toward transparent, scale chains | Price transparency compresses margins. ## 6. Key risks (ranked)

1. **Valuation compression** if FY27 growth slows toward the 7–8% market rate once acquisitions stop adding.
2. **Pressure on supplier income or pricing** (rebates/promotion are 55% of PBT; medicine price display; competition from Watsons, Guardian, Alpro, AA and online).
3. **Overhang** from Creador and the founders after the lock-up; a further sell-down via block placements.
4. **Integration and goodwill impairment** on RM1.34bn of intangibles; pharmacist availability for new stores.
5. **Rising rates** de-rating high-P/E defensives.

## 7. Client suitability and recommended exposure (65 years old, RM50m net worth)

**Fit.** At 65 the priorities are typically capital preservation, liquidity and income, with a 20+ year horizon that still needs some growth. A single newly listed stock at ~40× earnings, yielding 1.3%, with negative NTA, is a **satellite or opportunistic** holding, not core. The sector is defensive, but the valuation is not. Conditions:

- **Risk profile must already permit** single-stock equity and IPO participation under the bank's suitability process (IPOs are usually rated high-risk). Document this.
- **Size:** bid **RM250k (0.5% of net worth)**. Expect a scale-back. Keep *all* IPO and single-stock "special situations" within ~2–3% of investable assets.
- **Money:** it must come from the growth/opportunistic sleeve, not the income bucket. Cash must be ready for the 16/11 payment.
- **Cost:** the 2.5% fee + SST = **2.7% (RM6,750 on a full fill)**. The listing gain has to exceed ~2.9% (including exit costs) before the trade makes money. Buying in the secondary market instead avoids the fee but means paying any first-day premium.

## 8. Check before submitting the bid (final prospectus / term sheet)

- **Price.** Final retail and institutional prices. A private-bank bid usually goes through the institutional bookbuild, whose price can be **above** the RM1.08 indication; the retail price is capped. **Do not bid above RM1.08 without re-running the numbers.**
- **Earnings.** **FY26 PATAMI** (audited). It should be ≥ ~RM190m; if lower, the P/E is above 42× and the case weakens.
- **Proceeds and debt.** Use-of-proceeds amounts (debt repayment vs distribution centre) and pro forma net debt.
- **Cornerstones.** Names, size, and whether they are locked up.
- **Shariah status** (it affects local institutional demand) and **dividend timing** under the up-to-50% payout policy.
- **Related-party transactions and minority interests** (14.5% of FY25 PAT leaked to minorities).
- **Fee basis.** Whether the 2.7% fee applies to the allotted amount only.
- **Conflict check.** HLB group is not an adviser or underwriter (Maybank IB, RHB IB and AmInvestment Bank are). The fee is earned by the distributing bank; disclose it per policy.
