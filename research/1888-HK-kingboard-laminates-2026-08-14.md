# PART A — FUNDAMENTAL RESEARCH BRIEF

## INVESTMENT THESIS

**1. The earnings inflection is real, already visible in the parent's disclosure, and not yet in a single reported 1888 financial statement.** Kingboard Holdings (0148.HK), which consolidates this business, disclosed in late July 2026 that copper-clad laminate segment profit rose **more than 200% year-on-year in H1 2026**. Kingboard Laminates' own last reported half — H1 2025 — was revenue of HK$9.59bn (+11% YoY) and EPS of HK$0.30. The market is being asked to price a business whose core segment tripled its profit on a set of accounts that will not be published until later this month. That gap between disclosed operating reality and reported financials is the single most exploitable feature of this name right now.

**2. The stock trades at roughly half the forward multiple of its closest listed comparable, on a cycle that industry research expects to run into 2027.** 1888 trades at a **forward P/E of 24.6x** against **Elite Material (2383.TW) at 47.1x forward** and **Shengyi Technology (600183.SS) at 91.6x trailing**. Standard FR-4 CCL spot prices moved from roughly RMB70 per sheet in July 2025 to **RMB260 per sheet by June 2026 (+270%)**, and China Merchants Securities has publicly framed the CCL price upcycle as extending conservatively into H1 2027. 1888 is the highest-volume, lowest-multiple way to own that pricing curve.

**3. Pricing power is being exercised, repeatedly and on the record.** Kingboard Laminates issued a fresh price increase notice to customers on **6 July 2026 raising FR-4 and other grades by up to 15%**, taking the cumulative increase across this cycle to **53%**. This is a company raising list prices into a shortage of upstream electronic glass fibre cloth and copper foil — not one defending share on price.

**Counter-weight to all three:** trailing P/E is **49.5x**, gross margin is **19.6%**, and ROIC is **12.2%**. This is a commodity-weighted manufacturer being valued on a pricing spike. The thesis is a cycle-timing thesis, not a compounding-quality thesis, and it must be sized as such.

## BUSINESS MODEL

Kingboard Laminates manufactures copper-clad laminate — the fibreglass-and-copper substrate that printed circuit boards are etched from. Revenue is essentially unit volume times laminate ASP, with the spread against three input costs: copper foil, electronic-grade glass fibre cloth, and epoxy resin. It has been among the world's largest CCL manufacturers by volume for close to two decades.

**The moat is vertical integration, and it is genuinely defensible.** Kingboard produces its own upstream inputs rather than buying them at spot. In a cycle where glass fibre cloth and copper foil are the binding constraints — which is precisely this cycle — an integrated producer captures the input-cost inflation as internal margin instead of paying it away. That is why segment profit tripled while revenue grew far less. Non-integrated CCL converters are getting squeezed in the same tape.

**What is commodity, and this matters:** the bulk of Kingboard's volume is standard FR-4 — general-purpose laminate for consumer electronics and mainstream computing. FR-4 is a specification, not a brand. Kingboard's advantage there is scale and cost position, not switching cost or design-in lock-in.

**Competitive position.** Industry analyst Prismark ranked **Elite Material (2383.TW) as the world's number one CCL manufacturer by 2025 revenue, displacing both Shengyi Technology and Kingboard.** Elite Material held **38.6% of the global high-speed CCL market as of Q2 2026**. That is the competitive fact that most matters here: the premium tier — the low-loss, high-speed grades qualified into AI server boards and next-generation GPU platforms, which command **30–50% price premiums** — is where the durable margin sits, and Kingboard is under-indexed to it relative to Elite Material. Kingboard's leverage in this cycle is to the FR-4 *shortage*, which is a tightness story. Elite Material's leverage is to *qualification*, which is a franchise story. Those decay differently when capacity arrives.

Named peer set: Elite Material (2383.TW), Shengyi Technology (600183.SS), Taiwan Union Technology (6274.TWO), Nan Ya PCB (8046.TW), Doosan Corp (000150.KS).

## RISKS

**Elite Material is winning the tier that survives the cycle.** Prismark ranked Elite Material the world's largest CCL maker by 2025 revenue, ahead of both Kingboard and Shengyi, and Elite Material held **38.6% of global high-speed CCL as of Q2 2026**. High-speed grades qualified into AI server boards carry **30–50% price premiums** and, critically, carry design-in qualification cycles that make share durable. Kingboard's earnings leverage in this cycle runs through FR-4 tightness instead. *Mechanism:* when the industry's announced capacity expansions land — commodity FR-4 lines are the fastest to build and the first to arrive — FR-4 ASPs normalise while high-speed grades stay tight. Kingboard's blended ASP and its 19.6% gross margin compress; Elite Material's do not. This hits the revenue line and the gross margin line simultaneously.

**The pricing spike is the earnings, and spikes mean-revert.** FR-4 went from RMB70 to RMB260 per sheet in eleven months. Nothing in that series is structural. A move back to RMB150 — still more than double the July 2025 level — would likely halve the incremental profit, taking FY26 EPS toward HK$1.10–1.20. At a de-rated 18x that is **HK$20–22 per share**, or roughly 45% below spot. This is the bear case and it does not require anything to go wrong operationally.

**Parent overhang is demonstrated, not hypothetical.** Kingboard Holdings placed HK$11.77bn of 1888 stock in June 2026 at an **11.5% discount** to market. A controlling shareholder that has shown willingness to monetise into strength at a double-digit discount is a live supply risk on any subsequent rally. Assume it recurs.

**The investment segment injects earnings volatility unrelated to laminates.** The parent's investment division swung to a HK$500m loss in H1 2026. 1888's own FY2025 profit beat was helped by gains on equity investments. This means a portion of reported earnings is mark-to-market on a securities book, not laminate operations — and it can reverse in either direction without warning. It also makes the trailing 49.5x P/E a lower-quality anchor than it appears.

**Dividend cover is thinner than the payout ratio suggests.** 79% payout against FCF of HK$883m — i.e. dividends of roughly HK$2.1bn against HK$0.9bn of free cash flow in FY25. That gap is funded from the balance sheet. Sustainable while the cycle runs; not sustainable through a downturn alongside the capex programme.

5. **Gross margin below 18%** at the interim, which would signal input costs outrunning ASPs despite vertical integration.
