## INVESTMENT THESIS

- **Micron is running at an earnings level the memory industry has never seen, and it is contracted, not spot-driven.** Fiscal Q3 2026 (quarter ended May 2026, reported June 24, 2026) revenue was a record $41.46B, up 346% YoY and 74% sequentially, at an 84.9% non-GAAP gross margin — and roughly $100B of binding multi-year AI memory contracts (16 long-term agreements, the latest with Anthropic at ~$22B through 2027–28) have effectively sold out HBM supply into 2027. - **The market is paying a cyclical multiple for what management frames as structural demand — that gap is the trade.** At ~$905 the stock trades at ~12.4x FY2026E non-GAAP EPS (~$73.09, sum of three reported quarters plus Q4 guidance midpoint) and ~7.3x the annualized fiscal Q4 guide ($31.00/quarter). - **Cash generation has transformed the balance sheet into a weapon.** Fiscal Q3 alone produced $25.39B operating cash flow and $18.3B adjusted free cash flow against total debt of just $5.7B and $30.2B cash — a ~$24.5B net cash position. Micron can outspend any prior downcycle playbook (capex, buybacks, or both) without leverage.

## BUSINESS MODEL

Micron makes memory and storage semiconductors: DRAM (working memory — including high-bandwidth memory, HBM, the stacked DRAM bolted onto AI accelerators) and NAND (persistent flash storage). It is one of only three companies on earth that can manufacture leading-edge DRAM at scale, alongside Samsung Electronics and SK Hynix; Micron has historically held roughly a quarter of global DRAM bit share (third position, per TrendForce data through 2025). In fiscal Q3 2026, DRAM was $31.3B of revenue — 76% of the total, up 343% YoY — and data-center revenue exceeded $25B (a >$100B annualized run rate). Reporting segments: Cloud Memory $13.77B, Core Data Center $11.52B, Mobile & Client $11.52B, Automotive & Embedded $4.63B.

**The moat:** process technology (three-player oligopoly with brutal capital and know-how barriers — a new entrant needs ~a decade and tens of billions), and, newly, *qualification lock-in*: HBM must be co-designed and qualified with each accelerator (NVIDIA and others), which converts a commodity business into a design-win business with multi-year contracted pricing. **What remains commodity:** conventional DRAM and NAND outside the AI stack, where China's CXMT is adding trailing-edge supply and pricing power depends on industry discipline.

## RISKS

- **Supply response — Samsung specifically.** The July 7 selloff mechanism: Samsung's results signaled aggressive capacity/capex expansion. New DRAM wafer capacity landing in 2027–28 hits *contract renewal pricing* first; at a $50B/quarter revenue run rate, each 10 points of gross-margin compression removes ~$5B of quarterly gross profit (~$4/quarter of EPS). Samsung has historically been willing to burn industry margin to regain share.
- **Pricing second derivative already rolling.** +93–98% QoQ (Q1) → +58–63% (Q2) per TrendForce. If quarterly contract price growth reaches zero while capacity grows, revenue can decline even with unit demand intact — this is how every prior memory cycle ended.
- **China/CXMT in commodity DRAM.** CXMT capacity additions pressure non-AI DRAM (Mobile & Client is $11.5B/quarter of revenue), capping the segment that isn't protected by HBM qualification moats.
- **Customer concentration / contract opacity.** A handful of hyperscalers and NVIDIA drive data-center demand ($25B+/quarter). Contract terms (price re-openers, take-or-pay strength) are not publicly disclosed — **data unavailable** — so the "sold out through 2027" claim cannot be independently stress-tested.
- **Peak-multiple-on-peak-earnings trap.** Paying 12x FY26E assumes structural change; if FY28 EPS mean-reverts toward even $40–50, today's price implies ~20x mid-cycle earnings for a cyclical.

**Invalidation triggers (observable, specific):** (1) first *negative* QoQ TrendForce conventional DRAM contract-price print; (2) FQ1-27 guidance (September call) implying sequential gross-margin decline >300bp; (3) Samsung + SK Hynix combined CY2027 capex guidance up >30% YoY; (4) evidence of Micron losing HBM4 share at NVIDIA to SK Hynix or Samsung; (5) technical: a close below ~$790 (see Part B) would signal institutional distribution inconsistent with the thesis.
