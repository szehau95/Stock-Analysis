## 2. Which column is the fair benchmark?

Four different answers exist depending on what you adjust for. For a Malaysian investor spending ringgit, the **last column is the honest comparison**:

| Column | What it measures | Use when |
|---|---|---|
| S&P 500 (USD) | Gross total return, index terms | Comparing index to index |
| S&P 500 (MYR) | Above, converted at spot USD/MYR | You hold USD assets and spend MYR |
| **S&P 500 (MYR, net WHT)** | **Above, less 30% US dividend withholding** | **You are a Malaysian tax resident** |

Malaysia has **no tax treaty with the US**, so US dividends paid to a Malaysian individual are withheld at **30%** with no reclaim. That costs roughly 0.5% p.a. at current S&P yields. Malaysian dividends are single-tier and **tax-exempt** in the hands of individuals — no equivalent drag on Maybank.

**Over ten years the share price rose 35.9% — a 3.1% CAGR.** Everything else was dividends and their compounding. Maybank is an income instrument that happens to be listed as equity; it is not a capital growth story, and the 10-year record says so unambiguously.

The compounding matters: RM 5.80 of dividends per share over ten years, reinvested, turned 1 share into 1.88 shares. Taken as cash and spent, the same investor would have RM 5.80 + RM 2.88 of price gain = RM 8.68 on RM 8.02 (+108%), not +155.5%. **Reinvestment added 47 percentage points.**

## 5. Window sensitivity — read the 5-year number carefully

Maybank's apparent win over 5 years is largely a starting-point artefact:

- The **5-year window starts August 2021** — Maybank at RM 8.01, still depressed from COVID and its FY2020 dividend cut, while the S&P had already completed a violent recovery rally. Maybank starts cheap, the S&P starts expensive.
- The **10-year window starts August 2016** — Maybank at RM 8.02, essentially the *same price*. Ten years of price action produced RM 2.88 per share.

Maybank's price was RM 8.02 in Aug 2016 and RM 8.01 in Aug 2021. **The stock went nowhere for five years.** The 5-year "outperformance" is measured from the bottom of that flat stretch; the 10-year figure spans the whole of it. The 10-year number is the more representative one.

## 6. What this says about the leveraged version

Cross-reference: `maybank-margin-risk-analysis.md`.

The 10-year record makes the margin case worse, not better:

| | Value |
|---|---|
| Maybank 10-year CAGR | **9.84%** |
| Of which price | 3.1% |
| Of which dividends | ~6.7% |
| Margin borrowing cost | ~5.5% |
| **Spread over funding** | **~4.3% p.a.** |

Leverage on a 9.84% asset at 5.5% funding is not free money — it is a 4.3-point spread bought with a 2.5x multiplier on a stock whose median year draws down 13.5%. And because **77% of the return is dividends**, the strategy depends on the payout holding. Maybank cut it in FY2020 (RM 0.665 → RM 0.525 across the FY2020 declarations), which is precisely when a leveraged holder would also have been facing margin calls.

Note too that this 10-year total return **includes the reinvestment compounding**. A margin investor typically uses the dividends to service interest rather than reinvest — which strips out the very component that produced 77% of the return. On that basis the realised return drops toward the RM 8.68-on-RM 8.02 figure (+108%, ~7.6% CAGR) before interest, and below the 5.5% funding cost once interest is paid on the borrowed half.

## 7. Bottom line

| Question | Answer |
|---|---|
| Maybank 3-year total return | **+44.7%** (13.12% p.a.) |
| Maybank 5-year total return | **+86.4%** (13.26% p.a.) |
| Maybank 10-year total return | **+155.5%** (9.84% p.a.) |
| Beat the S&P 500 (MYR, net)? | 3y ✗ · 5y ✓ · 10y ✗ |
| 10-year shortfall vs S&P | **−136 percentage points** |
| Share of return from dividends | 53% / 58% / **77%** |
| 10-year price CAGR alone | **3.1%** |

Maybank has been a solid income holding — roughly 10% a year over a decade with low-teens returns in the recent windows. But it has been a poor capital compounder, and against a US index benchmark in ringgit terms it has lost decisively over the full ten years. The single 5-year win comes from a favourable start date, not from structural outperformance.
